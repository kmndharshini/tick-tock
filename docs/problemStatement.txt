Problem Statement: Interactive Clock Learning App for Kids
1. Project title
Tick-Tock: An Interactive Clock Learning Game for Children
2. Problem statement
Many young children find it difficult to learn how to read analog clocks, identify the hour and minute hands, understand the relationship between hours and minutes, and convert analog time into digital time. Traditional teaching methods, such as textbooks and printed worksheets, may not provide enough hands-on practice to help children understand these concepts.

Therefore, there is a need for an interactive, child-friendly clock-learning application or website that supports touchscreen interaction. The application should allow children to learn by touching and moving clock hands, answering fun exercises, matching analog clocks with digital times, and receiving instant feedback on their answers.

The system should provide progressive learning activities, starting with basic hour recognition and advancing to half-past, quarter-past, quarter-to, five-minute intervals, and everyday time-related activities. Through visual demonstrations, interactive exercises, rewards, and simple instructions, the application aims to improve children's time-reading skills, confidence, and independent learning.

The website or app should work on touch-enabled devices such as tablets, touchscreen computers, and smartphones, as well as traditional computers using a mouse.

5. Touchscreen accessibility requirements
The application should include:
- Large, easy-to-tap buttons and clock hands.
- Drag-and-drop and tap-to-set interactions.
- Clear colors, readable text, and child-friendly illustrations.
- Audio instructions for children who are still learning to read.
- Immediate feedback, with hints instead of discouraging error messages.
- Keyboard and mouse support in addition to touch.
- Responsive layouts for phones, tablets, and touchscreen computers.
- Accessibility support for children with different learning needs.
6. Objectives of the project
1. Help children understand analog and digital clocks.
2. Improve time recognition through interactive practice.
3. Encourage independent learning through games and rewards.
4. Make clock learning engaging and accessible on touchscreens.
5. Track learning progress and gradually increase exercise difficulty.
7. What could make your project different?
Rather than simply copying an existing clock game, you could build a touch-first learning website with a guided exercise pathway. For example, a child completes the “Learn the hour hand” activity before unlocking “Half past,” then advances to matching games and real-life time challenges. Teachers or parents could optionally view progress.

Recommended starting point: Explore Learn Clock Time and TellingTime.io first. They are particularly useful references for touch interaction and varied learning exercises.
